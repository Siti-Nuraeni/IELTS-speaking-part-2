How Part 2 works

Part 2 lasts 3–4 minutes. The examiner hands you a task card, paper and a pencil.

1 minute to prepareRead the card and make notes. You can’t talk yet.
1–2 minutes to talkSpeak without interruption. The examiner stops you at 2 minutes.
1–2 follow-up questionsShort answers that round off the topic before Part 3.

Using your preparation minute

Write key words, not sentences. One line per bullet point, plus one or two “wow” words you want to use. Full sentences take too long and tempt you to read aloud.

Example notes: a person who inspired you

  • Mrs Lim, chemistry teacher, age 15
  • bad at science, ‘written it off’
  • experiments: burning note
  • stayed after school
  • nearly failing → top grade
  • ability isn’t fixed → confidence

A two-minute structure

TimeWhat to say
0:00–0:15Introduce the topic in one or two sentences.
0:15–1:15Cover the bullet points with real detail: names, times, a small story.
1:15–1:50The “explain” point. Give two reasons and develop them.
1:50–2:00Reflect: how you feel now, or what you learned.

The band descriptors used for grading

Examiners score four criteria equally. Your talks are graded against these official descriptors. Each criterion gets a whole band, and the overall score is their average, rounded to the nearest half band.

Show bands 3 to 0

Notes from the descriptors: a candidate must fully fit the positive features of the descriptor at a particular level, and the real test rates average performance across all three parts. A Part 2 grade here is an estimate of your level, not an official score.

Do

  • Keep talking until the examiner stops you. Aim for at least 1 minute 30 seconds.
  • Signpost: “The main reason…”, “Looking back…”, “As for how I felt…”.
  • Tell a small story with narrative tenses. Stories are easy to extend.
  • If your mind goes blank, describe a detail: what it looked like, who was there.

Avoid

  • Stopping after one minute. Short talks can’t show Band 7 fluency.
  • Reading your notes aloud or reciting a memorised speech.
  • Spending so long on the first bullet that you never reach “explain why”.
  • Long silences while you think. Use fillers like “Let me think…” naturally.

Cue cards & model answers

Give yourself a minute to prepare and try the card aloud before you reveal the model. Highlighted phrases are the Band 7–8 language worth borrowing.
